# Judging rule v2 (US market)

Effective from 2026-11-26. The machine-readable version is `v2.json`; if the two ever disagree, `v2.json` is what ran.
Records made under v1 keep v1. `v1.md` and `v1.json` are unchanged.

## What changed from v1, and why

v1 was run over data from 1990 onward (see `backtest/`). Two weaknesses showed up, and v2 changes exactly these two things:

1. **An inverted yield curve alone no longer forces Elevated.** Under v1, a yield curve at Extreme kept the
   state at Elevated for 613 trading days in a row from April 2022 to October 2024, while every other
   component had calmed down. The yield curve still counts toward the score like any other component.
2. **Stepping down needs confirmation.** Under v1 the state changed 543 times in 37 years, including
   one-day alerts. In v2 the state moves **up at once**, but moves **down only after the rule has indicated
   a lower state for 5 trading days in a row**, and then only to the highest state indicated in those days.

On the same past data, v2 changed state 276 times, produced no alert shorter than four days, and
entered Elevated and Stress on the same days as v1 in 2008, 2020 and 2022.

This comparison is hindsight. v2 was written after looking at v1's weaknesses, so it fits the past better
than it should be expected to fit the future. The ledger from 2026-11-26 onward is the real test.

## What the rule does

It describes the current risk condition of the US stock market using five components. It does not
predict prices.

Each component gets a level from 0 to 3: **Low, Moderate, High, Extreme**. Thresholds are the same as v1.

| Component | Input | Low | Moderate | High | Extreme |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equity volatility | VIX close | below 15 | 15 to 20 | 20 to 30 | 30 or more |
| Credit stress | US high-yield bond spread (percentage points) | below 3.5 | 3.5 to 5 | 5 to 7 | 7 or more |
| Yield curve | 10-year minus 3-month Treasury yield | 0.5 or more | 0 to 0.5 | -0.5 to 0 | below -0.5 |
| Financial conditions | Chicago Fed NFCI | below -0.5 | -0.5 to 0 | 0 to 0.5 | 0.5 or more |
| Equity drawdown | S&P 500 close, % below its highest close of the last 252 trading days | below 5 | 5 to 10 | 10 to 20 | 20 or more |

A value exactly on a boundary belongs to the higher-risk level.

## Score and indicated state

Score = 100 x (sum of the five levels) / 15, rounded.

| Score | Indicated state |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 24 | Calm |
| 25 to 49 | Watch |
| 50 to 74 | Elevated |
| 75 to 100 | Stress |

Floors: if any one component **other than the yield curve** is Extreme, the indicated state is at least
Elevated. If two or more of them are Extreme, it is Stress.

## Published state

- If the indicated state is higher than the published state, the published state moves up the same day.
- If the indicated state has been lower than the published state for 5 trading days in a row, the
  published state moves down to the highest state indicated in those 5 days.
- Otherwise it stays.

A trading day is a date on which a daily market input (VIX, high-yield spread, yield curve) has an
observation. The first v2 record starts from the last state published under v1.

Each record shows both: `state` (published) and `indicated_state` (what the rule said that day), plus
the indicated state of the last 5 trading days, so the confirmation step can be checked by hand.

## Alerts

An alert is recorded whenever the published state changes or any component changes level, compared
with the previous record.

## Known limits of v2

- The thresholds are still round numbers chosen by judgment.
- The state describes current conditions. In the backtest it reacted after declines had begun, not before.
- The backtest could use the S&P 500 only from late 2016 and the high-yield spread only from late 2023
  (source limits), so 2008 was judged on three components and 2020 and 2022 on four.
- Holding a state for 5 days means the published state can stay higher than the day's indicated state.
- Inputs lag by about one trading day; NFCI is weekly.
- No event information feeds the score; events are tracked separately in `events/`.

Any change to the above will be published as v3. This file will not be edited.
